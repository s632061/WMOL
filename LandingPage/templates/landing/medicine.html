WMOL PASS

Prepare medical learners for the environment, not just the material.

WMOL Pre-Matriculation Academic Success System (PASS) is a four-week pre-matriculation readiness program that bridges the gap for incoming medical learners and understanding the medical school environment, the habits needed, effective use of expert support, and tolerating its demands.

For institutions, PASS creates an early opportunity to support accepted learners through matriculation, recognize support needs sooner, improve learning efficiency, and reduce preventable remediation burden.

The Transition Problem

Where Academic Instability Begins

PASS gives learners a clear daily structure and how it relates to their future medical education rather than leaving them to prepare alone.

Institutional Value

What PASS Creates for the Institution

Support Accepted Learners Through Matriculation

PASS keeps accepted learners connected through structured academic activity, regular guidance, and a clear transition process. Rather than preparing alone, learners experience institutional level support before classes begin. This may reduce uncertainty, strengthen readiness, and support follow-through with matriculation at their chosen institution.

Create Earlier Opportunities for Support

PASS creates a lower-stakes period in which confusion, discouragement, avoidance, inconsistent engagement, or difficulty recovering can become visible. These patterns are not labels or predictions of failure. They give faculty and student-support teams an earlier opportunity to understand what a learner may need before small challenges become academic crises.

Improve Learning Efficiency

Learners often spend much of their available time collecting, rewriting, organizing, and reviewing information. PASS shifts more effort toward understanding, building usable memory, applying knowledge, correcting mistakes, and stabilizing performance. This allows for conversion of more study time into functional learning without simply asking learners to work longer.

Reduce Preventable Remediation Burden

Academic support is often activated after inefficient habits, discouragement, and performance instability have compounded. PASS introduces a usable learning system and gives learners a structured way to create sustainable habits, adjust to disruptions in daily life, and identify how to use expert support sooner. It augments faculty and student-support capacity by allowing a greater allocation of resources, time, energy, and mental bandwidth per student .

The Learning System

A Four-Part Repeatable Learning Process

PASS gives learners a repeatable system they can carry into medical school to sustain competency as demands rise and time and mental bandwidth shrinks, without losing their identity.

Understand

Learners identify what matters, determine which questions a topic must answer in real scenarios, and use available resources to build clear conceptual understanding.

Remember

Learners convert that understanding into compact, retrievable working memories that preserve the relationships and reasoning needed to rebuild the concept.

Apply

Learners use practice questions to test and refine their working memories. Mistakes show what must be clarified, added, or removed rather than serving only as scores.

Stay Consistent

Learners repeat the process under increasing volume, variation, and workload and learn which iteration of the learning system they want to use before medical school begins.

The Four-Week Program

Controlled Exposure Before the Consequences Rise

Each week changes the composition of the learner’s work, moving from guided understanding toward application, correction, and stable execution before full academic pressure begins.

Week 1

Understand and Build the Foundation

Learners learn the workflow, select resources, identify essential information, build conceptual understanding, and establish a manageable daily rhythm.

Week 2

Apply and Reveal Gaps

Learners begin consistent question-based application to determine whether understanding can be retrieved and used rather than merely recognized.

Week 3

Correct and Consolidate

Learners revisit missed questions, identify where their reasoning broke down, and refine incomplete working memories until weak areas become more reliable.

Week 4

Stabilize Under Simulated Conditions

Learners complete randomized sets combining prior and new material in order to build confidence and competence in simulated exam environments.

Implementation

Designed to Fit the Institution

Adapts to Existing Structures

PASS works around the institution’s curriculum, platforms, schedules, personnel, question banks, and learner-support resources. It does not require curricular redesign or platform replacement. The system can be configured to work with tools such as Lecturio when selected by the institution.

Includes Its Own Implementation System

PASS works around the institution’s curriculum, platforms, schedules, personnel, question banks, and learner-support resources. It serves as a preparatory and support layer rather than requiring curricular redesign or platform replacement. Institution-specific adaptations are made without changing the core learning process.

Developed Through High-Constraint Use

WMOL was developed and refined across demanding medical, academic, military, language-learning, endurance, and institutional environments. Although the content changed, each setting required execution under volume, fatigue, uncertainty, and constraint. That experience shaped the system’s emphasis on repeatability, recovery, and stable performance.

Designed to Scale Without Proportionally Increasing Faculty Burden

Standardized guidance, communications, learner workflows, and monitoring processes allow larger cohorts to be supported without recreating every interaction individually. Human support remains available where professional judgment matters most. PASS augments institutional capacity rather than replacing it.

Evidence and Risk Reduction

Current Use, Structured Evaluation, and Emerging Evidence

Institutional Use

IRB-Approved Pre-Matriculation Pilot

WMOL PASS is being implemented through an IRB-approved pre-matriculation pilot in collaboration with Touro University California, with participating learners from Touro’s California and Montana medical-school cohorts.

Research

Structured Evaluation

Evaluation focuses on feasibility, participation, learner experience, adoption of the learning process, recovery, early academic stability, and continued system use after matriculation.

Emerging Evidence

Findings Added as Analyses Are Completed

Analyses are in progress. Approved implementation findings, learner-reported outcomes, academic-stability measures, presentations, and publications will be added as they become available. Those findings will also guide future refinements while preserving the core learning process.
